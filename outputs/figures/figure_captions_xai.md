# Explanation figure captions

**Figure 9. Predictive performance.** (a) Validation AUC (Sep-Dec 2025) of untuned candidate algorithms on the main (filled) and origin-only (open) feature sets; LightGBM was selected for tuning. (b-d) ROC, precision-recall and decile calibration curves of the locked models on the 2026 test set (400,000 randomly drawn trips per model; dotted lines in (c) = observed e-bike share; bars in (d) = distribution of LightGBM predictions). (e) Test AUC by month. (f) Calibration intercept by month (positive = e-bike use under-predicted). (g) Test AUC with day-block bootstrap 95% CIs (200 replicates) on all 3.6 million test trips. (h) Confusion matrix of the main LightGBM model, row-normalized, at the threshold chosen on validation (0.45). (i) Optuna search: each trial's validation log loss (points) and best value so far (line). The origin-only model uses all dock-start trips, so its sample and base rate differ.

**Figure 10. Global explanation of the locked main LightGBM model.** (a) SHAP values (log-odds of choosing an e-bike) for the 15 most important features, 12,000 randomly drawn 2026 test trips; color = within-feature percentile of the feature value. (b) Importance of feature groups, as mean absolute grouped SHAP value and as the drop in test AUC when the group is permuted within month (5 repeats), each scaled to its maximum. (c) Mean |SHAP| in season-matched 2025 (Feb-Oct, training period) and 2026 (Jan-Oct, test) samples with day-block bootstrap 95% CIs. SHAP values describe model behavior, not causal effects.

**Figure 11. Accumulated local effects (ALE) of the locked main model.** Centered ALE on the log-odds scale for members (blue) and casual riders (orange), computed on season-matched 2025 (dashed) and 2026 (solid, day-block bootstrap 95% band) samples with 20 quantile bins (hourly bins for hour); the yellow area is the 2026 gap between members and casual riders. The mirrored strip under each panel shows the distribution of the feature for members (up) and casual riders (down); the x-axis spans its 1st-99th percentile. Because the model is fixed, differences between dashed and solid lines reflect where riders were in the joint feature space, not a re-estimated effect; changed effects are examined with twin models (Figure 16). Neighborhood measures take one value per census tract, so their ALE curves are step-like and partly capture place-specific differences between station areas rather than a smooth effect of the measure.

**Figure 12. Interactions in the locked main model.** (a) Mean absolute SHAP interaction value (both halves summed) among the 12 features with the largest total attribution, 3,000 2026 test trips (300 per month); the strongest pairs are labelled. (b) The ten largest pairwise interactions as a share of total absolute attribution; dots show the feature group of each partner. Main effects account for 32% of the total, so non-additive structure is substantial. (c-e) SHAP value of a feature against its value, colored by a second variable; lines are binned means. (f, g) Mean predicted e-bike probability by distance band and start hour for members and casual riders, and (h) by temperature and distance (all riders, %), on the 2026 explanation sample (95,000 trips; cells with fewer than 25 trips blank). These are conditional means of model output, not causal surfaces.

**Figure 13. Why did the e-bike share rise? Season-matched decomposition, 2025 to 2026.** Same months (February to October), each month weighted equally; 2025 trips are training data and 2026 trips test data, 10,000 trips per month and year. Because the model is fixed, the change in mean log-odds equals the sum of the changes in mean SHAP value of each feature. (a) Monthly contributions of feature groups (stacked), the part not explained (hatched) and the observed change (diamonds). (b) Cumulative contributions from 2025 to 2026 for all months together. (c) Share of the observed change explained by the main model and by the model with the 7-day station supply proxy (5% and 43% for all riders). (d) Group contributions and the unexplained change for members and casual riders. (e) The ten features with the largest contributions. (f) Standardized mean differences of features between the two years. Error bars: day-block bootstrap 95% CIs (500 replicates); gray band in (f) = |SMD| < 0.1. The supply proxy is the lagged e-bike share of departures at the station and carries both fleet availability and earlier rider choices; its share is an upper bound for availability.

**Figure 14. Where the 2026 under-prediction occurs.** All 3.6 million 2026 test trips with predictions from the locked main model. (a) Weekly observed (solid) and predicted (dashed) e-bike share for members and casual riders (weeks with at least 500 trips); the dotted line marks the March 16, 2026, price and fleet change. (b, c, e, f) Observed minus predicted share (percentage points) by trip distance, origin-tract median-income decile, start hour and month. (d) The same gap by origin census tract (at least 300 trips; color range clipped at +/-20 pp; gray = fewer trips). A broad, near-uniform positive gap points to a shift in the level of e-bike choice rather than a change confined to particular trips or places.

**Figure 15. Local explanations for 2026 test trips.** (a) Decision paths: each line follows one trip from the model's base value (dashed) through the summed contribution of the less important features and then the 12 most important features (bottom to top) to its final log-odds; 300 random 2026 trips are colored by their final output, and the eight selected cases are drawn in black and numbered. (b) SHAP values (log-odds) of the ten most important features for the eight cases, with the predicted probability and the observed bike (E = e-bike, C = classic). Cases: (1, 2) most confident correct e-bike and classic predictions, (3, 4) most confident false positive and false negative at the validation threshold (0.45), (5) closest to the threshold, (6) median prediction, (7) member on a long trip (top distance decile) and (8) casual rider on a short trip (bottom quintile), all distinct trips. (c-f) Waterfalls for cases 3, 4, 7 and 8 with the six largest contributions; the rest are summed.

**Figure 16. Stability, effect drift and the cost of drift (post hoc analyzes).** (a) Mean |SHAP| of the top 10 features for the locked model and five refits with different seeds (same 20,000 2026 trips). (b) AUC of twin models with identical settings trained on February-October 2025 or 2026 (every 5th day held out; equal training size; mean of 3 replicates with different seeds and 80% subsamples), evaluated on held-out days of each year. (c) Mean predicted e-bike share of each twin on the same held-out reference trips. (d, e) ALE of distance and temperature from each twin on the same reference trips; bands span the 3 replicates. (f) Monthly 2026 test log loss of the frozen locked model, the frozen model with its intercept re-estimated on the 28 days ending 7 days before the month, and a full refit on all trips up to 7 days before the month.
